ACT 06

What is real, and what is waiting

The part most documents like this leave out

Everything above works. But a system that works and a system that is ready are different things, and the gap is not code — it is information only the resort has.

The menu is mostly undecided

Of 29 sellable items, only 13 have been classified. The other 16 are still UNTRACKED, which means the system will refuse to sell them.

KindCountMeaning
RECIPE5Deducts each ingredient in its recipe
DIRECT2Deducts one of itself — a bottle, a can
SERVICE6Someone confirmed it consumes nothing
UNTRACKED16Nobody has decided yet — cannot be sold

This is not a fault to be fixed by a programmer. It is roughly an hour of decisions that only the head chef and the manager can make: how much fuel a boat trip burns, how much oil a massage uses, what goes into each dish.

Almost nothing has a cost yet

There are 33 ingredients on the system and exactly 1 of them knows what it costs — because exactly 1 purchase has ever been recorded.

Cost is not typed in; it is worked out from what was actually paid for deliveries. That is the right design — nobody can inflate a cost to hide a margin — but it means the profit screens stay blank until real purchases go in. Record a few weeks of deliveries and the entire menu-profit picture lights up on its own.

Why the screens say "cannot be measured" instead of guessing

A dish with no ingredient costs could be shown with an assumed margin. It would look better and it would be a lie — and someone would eventually drop a profitable dish because of it.

The system says it does not know. That is a feature, and it is the same instinct behind refusing to sell an unclassified item.

Three things deliberately not built

Not builtWhy not
Tax invoices (eTIMS) A tax invoice needs a serial number and identifiers issued once that must never change. The format is the accountant's decision. Choosing one now and changing it later would mean reissuing every invoice already handed to a guest.
Offline tills If two tablets take an order for the same table while the network is down, one of them has to lose. Who wins, and what the waiter sees, are decisions about how the floor runs — not something to guess at. Built on a guess, it would look like it worked and quietly lose orders.
Spoilage sensitivity The alert currently treats 10 litres of milk the same as 10 grams of saffron. Fixing it properly needs real volume history, which arrives with the purchase records above.

VAT is built, but unconfirmed

Prices are treated as including 16% VAT, which is the Kenyan hospitality norm. The rate is stored as a setting rather than written into the software, so it can be changed when the law changes without anyone touching code — and each sale keeps a copy of the rate that applied on the day.

That last detail matters: when the rate changes, last year's receipts must still show last year's rate. They do.

What remains is a question for the accountant, not the system: are these prices really VAT-inclusive, and which items are exempt or zero-rated?

METHOD

How this document was made

So you can judge whether to believe it

Every screenshot here came from the system running on a real machine, driven automatically, one screen at a time. Nothing was drawn or arranged.

There was an earlier attempt at this. It produced forty screenshots, and eight of them were the same image — the login screen — saved under eight different names: "manager cash", "manager menu", "manager staff", and five more. That run had navigated without ever logging in, and photographed whatever happened to be on the screen.

The rule that came out of it

The capture used for this document proves it is on the right screen before it takes the picture. If it cannot find something that belongs to that screen, it records a failure and moves on.

A missing screenshot is honest. A wrong screenshot with a confident caption is worse than nothing, because it is the kind of mistake nobody catches by reading.

Two screenshots came out identical, and one was deleted

"Schedule" and "Performance" produced byte-for-byte the same picture — because for a waiter they genuinely are the same screen: the access-restricted notice.

That is honest, but captioning one image as two different screens is exactly the mistake this document was written to avoid. One was deleted. It appears once, labelled as what it is.

The stock deduction was not taken on trust either

The capture recorded stock before and after but never actually sold anything in between, so both readings were identical — which proves nothing in either direction.

So one Grilled Tilapia was put through the live system by hand: ordered, sent, and marked ready by the head chef. The tilapia fillet fell from 7.2000 kg to 6.8500 kg. That is 0.3500 kg — the recipe line exactly. Those are the figures in Act 3, and they were produced that way.

This document has already been corrected once

The first published version explained the missing band-issue screenshot as a security measure — that staff must enter a PIN to start a shift before they can hand out wristbands. It read well and it was wrong.

There is no such rule. The page simply does not exist on that tablet, and the login screen is what the app shows for anything it does not recognise. The invented explanation was more flattering than the truth, which is exactly why it needed checking.

Corrected here rather than quietly edited out, because a document about not trusting confident captions should show its own.

The same principle applies to the numbers. Anything shown in this style was read from the running system, not written from memory.
