ACT 05

Every dashboard, and the question it answers

A screen that answers no question is a screen nobody opens

There are three separate apps, and which one a person gets is decided by their job, not by a setting they can change. A waiter cannot reach the owner's app by typing a different address — the system checks who they are on every single request, not just when they log in.

That last point matters more than it sounds. If a person is dismissed at 2pm, their access dies at 2pm, even if their phone still has them logged in.

The audit screen deserves a paragraph

Every action writes a line, and each line carries a fingerprint of the line before it. Remove or edit any entry and every fingerprint after it stops matching.

You cannot quietly delete an inconvenient row. You can only add. That is what makes the answer to "who voided that order at 9pm?" worth having.

The captured screen holds 1,510 entries and reads like a diary of the day: ivan.kipchoge order.send, hassan.omondi gate.issue_band band=17, three clock-ins with the tablet's address, a login.

Follow that band number across this document. Here in the audit log, band=17 is recorded as issued by hassan.omondi. On the gate screen in Act 1, band #17 appears again in the recent list, now showing KSh 4,200 owed after the tilapia charged to it.

Two screens, written by different parts of the system, describing the same wristband at two moments in its life. Nobody linked them. They agree because neither is storing its own copy of the truth.

Who is allowed to set a price

The head chef owns recipes, ingredients, dishes and their prices — for the kitchen and the bar, because a cocktail is a recipe like any other.

The manager owns the service catalogue and prices across every department — the spa treatments, the boat rides, the day passes.

These do not overlap, and the boundary is enforced. A chef cannot reclassify a spa treatment as a kitchen item in order to take control of it.

What ordinary staff see

Four screens, deliberately. A waiter does not need a dashboard; they need to clock in, see their shifts, read what they have been sent, and know how they are doing.

Follow the fish one last time

That inbox reads: "Order ready — Band #20. 1.00× Grilled Tilapia is ready for pickup." And again for bands #19, #18 and #17.

Those are the same tilapia dishes that appear on band #20's bill in Act 2, that moved 0.35 kg of fish out of the store room in Act 3, and that left band #17 owing KSh 4,200 on the gate screen in Act 1.

One chef tapping "ready" charged a guest, cleared a kitchen ticket, deducted stock, wrote an audit line, and told the waiter to collect — and every screen in this document is looking at that single event from a different chair.

Two doors, not one

Look at the menu down the left of that screenshot: Clock, Alerts, Profile, Calendar, Incident. That is everything this waiter can reach. There is no Schedule, no Manager, no Finance — the menu shows only what the person can actually use.

The screenshot itself is the second door. It is what happens if someone types the address anyway, or follows an old bookmark. The menu hides it; the guard refuses it.

And it says so plainly — "You don't have permission to view this page" with a way back. An earlier version showed a blank page or told people to check their internet connection, which sent staff chasing a network fault that did not exist.

A bug worth remembering from the clock screen

For a period, the "Clock Out" button clocked people in. The button knew what it was called but the request underneath it was hard-wired the wrong way.

Nobody could end a shift, and because pay is worked out from these records, it was a payroll problem rather than a cosmetic one. It is fixed — the direction is now decided once, when the button is tapped, and carried through.

The station tablets

These are the fixed screens on the resort floor — the gate, the bar, the kitchen pass, the spa desk, the front desk. They hold the fewest screens of the three apps on purpose.

The station app is not allowed to reach the finance screens at all. Not hidden from them — unable to reach them. A till on a public counter is the easiest device in the resort to walk up to, so it carries the least.
