01 — How to read this

Nothing here is described from a drawing

Every screen below was opened and used before it was written about, and photographed as the person whose job it is — the gate screen as the gate lead, the kitchen board as the head chef, the clock as a waiter. Each caption names the address and the account underneath it.

Four kinds of checking sit behind this document, and they catch different things. Automated tests hold the rules: that money is never a rounded number, that a payment cannot be recorded twice, that a disabled account stops working on the very next request. A simulated working day runs the whole property end to end — gate opens, guests arrive, orders are sent, the kitchen cooks, cash is counted, the day closes — and then closes the books and audits itself. The screens themselves were opened in a browser and used, because a test can tell you an endpoint answers and still not tell you the button that calls it is reachable by the person who needs it.

And every photograph here was taken by a process that had to prove which screen it was on first. Each capture waits for a phrase that appears on that screen and nowhere else, checks it has not been bounced to a login page, and only then saves the image. If it cannot prove it, no image is saved and the miss is recorded. That rule exists because an earlier run once produced eight identical login screenshots filed under eight different names.

The fault this system hunts hardest

It is not a crash. It is a screen that opens for somebody and then fills with controls the system will refuse them — which teaches staff the software is unreliable and that the way round it is to stop using it.

Four of those were found during this review, by opening every screen as every role and recording what each one asked the system for: a waiter typing the manager's address got the manager's inbox, which answered refused and rendered “Nothing yet”; the menu manager opened for him with a price and a Remove on every row, none of which would have worked; the stock screen showed the head chef “No pending requests” when he was in fact being refused; and both kitchen and bar boards opened for six roles who do not work those posts. All four are closed. 296 role-and-screen combinations are now checked in a single run, and it currently reports none.

On the hardware you already have

Every screen is built to work on a phone, a tablet, a laptop, a desktop monitor, and a large counter touchscreen — the shape a till usually takes here. The counter case is the one most systems miss: it is a wide screen still operated by a finger, so every control is sized for a thumb at 1920px exactly as it is at 390px.

07 — Taking money

Cash, M‑Pesa, card — and the M‑Pesa prompt

Three ways to pay, at every selling point: the gate, the restaurant and bar, the spa and the water post, the villa folio and check-in. Nothing else is offered, because a method staff cannot explain produces payments nobody can verify.

M‑Pesa, by prompt

The till pushes a prompt to the guest's own phone. They enter their PIN, Safaricom confirms it back, and the payment arrives already matched against the statement. Nothing is typed in from a screen the till cannot see.

M‑Pesa, by code

For a guest who already paid the paybill themselves, a field for the code from their confirmation SMS. Optional on purpose — the guest is standing there and the SMS can be slow — but without it that payment cannot be matched at close of day, and the field says so.

Card

Recorded with the slip number so it can be matched against the card settlement. The swipe machine stays exactly as it is; the system records the payment and reconciles it.

Cash

Counted, handed over, and reconciled against what the system says that person took. Cash is the only one that needs a human to reconcile it — M‑Pesa and card match themselves.

What the prompt needs from you

Sending a prompt requires Safaricom Daraja credentials issued to the registered business — I cannot obtain them on your behalf. Until they are in place the till says so in plain words and falls back to the code field; it never offers a prompt it cannot send. The card and bank paths work the same way: built, dormant, and activated by adding credentials rather than by writing code.

08 — The money rules

One account, spent all over the property

These were decided by you during the build and are now enforced in the code, not left to memory. Each one says what came back when it was tried.

Spend all over, pay at any point

Any till
Does
A villa guest orders lunch, a spa client charges a massage, a swimmer buys a jet ski — all onto the account they already hold, settled when it suits them.
Shows
The till sees the whole bill — every department's charges, what is cleared, the balance — and for a wristband the credit left.
Checked
A water attendant opened Band #5 and sold a jet ski at 3,500: the 3,000 prepaid was consumed and 500 left owing, confirmed in the ledger. A waiter charged lunch to Villa 1 and the room balance moved 70,000 → 71,800.

A room is settled at front house

Your rule
Does
A wristband is settled wherever the guest is standing. A stay is settled at front house, where the deposit was taken and check-out happens.
Shows
At a till, a room account shows its bill and points at the desk rather than offering a payment. At the desk, the full folio with a control to take payment.
Checked
A waiter taking a payment against a villa was refused: “A room account is settled at front house.” Front desk settled the same folio — 100,000 charged, 30,000 deposit, 70,000 taken, balance 0 — and only then would check-out complete.

A band closes only when it is clear

Gate
Does
Ends the day's account when the guest leaves — and refuses to end one that still owes.
Shows
The band, what is outstanding, and a refusal naming the amount if anything is.
Checked
A band owing KSh 1,400 was refused: “Band #7 still owes KSh 1,400.00. Take the payment first — then close the band.” This was found open during review — the button closed a band owing 500 under a screen that said “Outstanding balance”.

Every bill belongs to somebody

Floor
Does
Removes the anonymous table. To order, a guest holds a wristband or is staying in a villa — two accounts that already exist before anyone orders, because the gate took the entry fee for one and front desk took a deposit for the other.
Shows
“Open a table” asks for the wristband number. A manager may still open one by hand for a lost band or a gate tablet that is down, and that exception carries their name.
Checked
A waiter opening a free-text table was refused: “Every bill belongs to a wristband or a room.” Fifteen such nameless accounts were found open during the review, one holding KSh 1,800 from the previous day.

Spending authority is delegated by budget

You set · the manager spends
Does
You set what a department may spend in a month. Inside that, a manager approves and gets on with it. Beyond it, the decision comes to you.
Shows
A manager approving past the line is told exactly where the line is. With no budget set, nothing is delegated. A manager may always reject — refusing spends nothing — and may never approve their own request, whatever the amount.
Checked
Bar set at 120,000. Manager approved a 6,600 request — approved. Manager tried 132,000 — refused: “KSh 80,160.00 is left of KSh 120,000.00 this month and this request is KSh 132,000.00. Only the owner can approve it.” A manager setting their own budget was refused.

Stock speaks three languages

Buying · counting · cooking
Does
Bought in crates and cases, counted in the item's own unit, cooked in portions. The system does the multiplication and works out what each bottle cost.
Shows
A unit switch on the quantity with the conversion stated on screen, the converted figure, and the unit cost against what it cost last time. In the kitchen, each ingredient in its portion unit with a live cost preview.
Checked
Received 6 crates: the screen read “1 crate = 25 bottle → 150 bottle” and “KSh 132 per bottle · was 132.2”. 1 kg is now 7 onions, 8 tomatoes, 12 limes, 5 tilapia fillets; a spirit bottle is 750 ml. Changing a portion definition rewrites every recipe written against it, so nothing silently starts consuming a different amount.

Villa rates were checked against your own website

Every nightly rate in the system matched. Two capacities did not: Villas 14 and 15 sleep eight, and the system had them at four. Corrected through the manager's own screen — which also revealed that saving a capacity change was being refused because the form sent the rate along with it. Rates are yours alone to set.

09 — Underneath

The rules that hold whatever anyone types

These are not features you switch on. They are properties of the system, and they are the reason the numbers above can be trusted.

Live figures are derived, never stored

Stock is the sum of every movement. A bill is charges minus payments. There is no “current balance” field for anyone to edit — the number is recalculated from the record every time it is read.

History is frozen where it happened

The price on a line of an old order is the price charged that day, kept with the line. Changing a menu price tomorrow does not rewrite last week's takings.

Nothing is deleted

Every record is disabled rather than removed. A staff member who leaves, a menu item withdrawn, a supplier dropped — all stay in the history already written.

A double tap cannot become a double charge

Every action carries a marker that makes repeating it harmless, enforced underneath the app and not only by the screen. A retried payment on a flaky connection lands once.

Permission is re-checked on every request

Signing in is not a pass for the day. The person's role and whether they are still active are re-read on each action, so disabling an account stops it on the next tap.

Everything that can stay open has something that closes it

Wristbands, shifts, tabs and leave requests all have a closing force that is not a human remembering — the roster closes shifts, stale tabs are raised, pending leave is chased daily and escalated to you after a week.

The clock belongs to the system

Times are stamped by the system itself, never taken from the tablet. A tablet with the wrong date cannot move a sale into yesterday.

Money is never a rounded number

Money is held exactly, to the cent, everywhere it travels. Shillings do not quietly disappear a cent at a time through rounding.

10 — Your data

Everything in this document is practice data

Every figure you have seen — the revenue, the twelve staff, Grilled Tilapia at 1,800, the Mwangi Family in Villa 1 — is invented. It is there so the screens could be photographed doing their job. None of it is Waterfront Juja, and none of it carries over.

Filling the system with your real resort is not a job you hand over and wait on. Almost all of it is done on the screens themselves, by your own people, and it only has to be done once. The system tells you what is still missing rather than quietly going along with it: the payroll sheet prints “no wage set” beside anyone without a rate, the budget tile stays blank until you set budgets, and a dish nobody has given a recipe cannot be sold at all until somebody says how it draws on stock.

Menu, prices and recipes

Your head chef adds each dish, its price and its department, and says how it draws on stock. The recipe is what makes the figures on your Profit screen real.

Villas and rates

Each villa, what it sleeps, its nightly rate, and whether it is in service.

Suppliers

Who you buy from, so every delivery recorded is recorded against somebody.

Staff, roles and wage rates

Each person, their role and department, their PIN, and their rate. Payroll drafts from the clock records the moment a rate is set.

Monthly budgets

A spending limit per department, per month. The budget-burn tile stays blank until you set them.

Opening stock

One physical count on the day you start. Everything after that is the system's own running total.

The two things that cannot come from a screen

Your staff network. The resort's own WiFi name and the range of addresses it hands out, registered once in Settings. Until it is, clock-in refuses everybody — deliberately, because being on the property's own network is what makes attendance mean anything.

M‑Pesa business credentials. Safaricom issues these to the registered business, so they have to come from you. They switch on the phone prompt and the automatic matching. Everything else about M‑Pesa — taking the payment, recording the code, reconciling it — works without them.

How the first week actually goes

I sit with your manager and we fill it in together, screen by screen, from whatever you already keep — a book, a spreadsheet, or a stack of supplier invoices. Then the system runs alongside what you do now for a week before anyone relies on it, so if the two disagree we find out while it still costs nothing.
